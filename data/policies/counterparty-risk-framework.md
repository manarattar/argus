# ARGUS Counterparty Risk Framework (CRF)

> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION POLICY.** This framework was written for the ARGUS
> demonstration corpus. It is not the policy of any institution, is not based on
> any institution's internal documents, and has no regulatory standing. Clause
> numbering exists so that policy citations in the demonstration are
> deterministic and checkable.

Version 4.2 | Owner: Group Credit Risk | Effective 1 January 2026

<!-- page: 1 -->

## 1. Purpose and Scope

**Clause reference: CRF 1.1**

This framework governs the periodic review of corporate counterparties. It
applies to every counterparty with aggregate exposure above EUR 1 million and to
every counterparty designated as strategically significant, irrespective of
exposure.

**Clause reference: CRF 1.2**

Reviews are conducted annually as a minimum. A shorter cycle is required where
clause CRF 7.1 applies.

## 2. Assessment Principles

**Clause reference: CRF 2.1**

Every conclusion recorded in a counterparty assessment must be traceable to
identified evidence. An assessment that records a conclusion without supporting
evidence is not complete and may not be approved.

**Clause reference: CRF 2.2**

Where evidence is insufficient to support a conclusion, the assessment must
record the insufficiency explicitly. Absence of adverse evidence must not be
recorded as evidence of absence of risk.

**Clause reference: CRF 2.3**

Analytical tooling, including automated or model-assisted analysis, may support
the preparation of an assessment. It may not determine the outcome. A named
analyst is accountable for every assessment and a named reviewer is accountable
for every approval.

## 3. Financial Assessment

<!-- page: 2 -->

**Clause reference: CRF 3.1**

Financial assessment must consider at least three reporting periods where
available, and must address profitability, leverage, liquidity and working
capital.

**Clause reference: CRF 3.2**

A counterparty whose net debt to EBITDA ratio exceeds 2.0x is classified as
elevated financial leverage and requires the additional analysis set out in the
Financial Resilience Standard.

**Clause reference: CRF 3.3**

An increase in net debt to EBITDA of more than 1.0x within a single reporting
period is a review trigger, irrespective of the absolute level reached.

**Clause reference: CRF 3.4**

Where a counterparty operates under financial covenants, the assessment must
record the covenant levels, the most recent test results, and the headroom under
the counterparty's own stated downside scenario.

## 4. Concentration

<!-- page: 3 -->

**Clause reference: CRF 4.1**

The assessment must record the proportion of the counterparty's revenue
attributable to its largest customer and to its five largest customers.

**Clause reference: CRF 4.2**

Where a single customer accounts for more than 25% of a counterparty's revenue,
or where the two largest customers together account for more than 50% of
revenue, the counterparty is classified as having material revenue
concentration. Material revenue concentration is a review trigger and requires
the assessment to consider the contractual security of the concentrated revenue.

**Clause reference: CRF 4.3**

Where revenue subject to material concentration is governed by a contract
expiring within twelve months of the assessment date and no signed renewal is in
place, this must be recorded as a specific risk and the assessment must state
the effect of non-renewal on the counterparty's covenant position.

**Clause reference: CRF 4.4**

Supply-side concentration must be assessed where the counterparty's operations
depend on single-sourced inputs. The assessment must record requalification lead
time and any mitigating inventory position.

## 5. Governance

<!-- page: 4 -->

**Clause reference: CRF 5.1**

The assessment must record board composition, the proportion of independent
directors, and the identity of the party nominating the chair.

**Clause reference: CRF 5.2**

A counterparty whose board contains no independent director, or whose audit
committee met fewer than four times in the preceding financial year, is
classified as having a governance deficiency. A governance deficiency is a
review trigger.

**Clause reference: CRF 5.3**

The absence of an internal audit function at a counterparty with revenue above
EUR 100 million must be recorded and assessed. Where an announced remediation is
relied upon, the assessment must state whether it has been demonstrated in
operation or is planned only.

## 6. Evidence and Documentation

**Clause reference: CRF 6.1**

Each assessment must record the documents examined and, where a conclusion rests
on a specific statement, the location of that statement within the source
document.

**Clause reference: CRF 6.2**

Where the assessment identifies information that a complete file would contain
but which was not available, the gap must be recorded together with its effect
on the conclusions drawn.

## 7. Escalation and Approval

<!-- page: 5 -->

**Clause reference: CRF 7.1**

An assessment resulting in an overall rating of High must be escalated to a
senior reviewer before approval, and the review cycle for that counterparty
shortens to six months.

**Clause reference: CRF 7.2**

An assessment identifying a potential breach of this framework or of any
subordinate standard must be escalated to a senior reviewer irrespective of the
overall rating.

**Clause reference: CRF 7.3**

Where a finding of Major or Severe severity rests on evidence assessed as weak
or insufficient, the assessment must be escalated rather than approved. The
evidential gap, not the rating, is the matter requiring senior judgement.

**Clause reference: CRF 7.4**

Where an analyst departs from a recommendation produced by analytical tooling,
the original recommendation, the analyst's decision and the reason for the
departure must all be retained in the assessment record.
