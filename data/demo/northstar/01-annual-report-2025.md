## 1. About Northstar Manufacturing

Northstar Manufacturing B.V. designs and assembles precision thermal-management
components for industrial and automotive customers. The company was founded in
2009, is headquartered in Eindhoven, the Netherlands, and operates two
production facilities: Eindhoven (assembly, final test) and Ostrava, Czech
Republic (machining, sub-assembly).

As at 31 December 2025 the company employed 612 people (2024: 548). The company
is privately held. The founding family retains 54% of ordinary shares through
Stichting Northstar Holding; the balance is held by Meridian Growth Partners
IV LP, which invested in 2021.

## 2. Chief Executive's Review

<!-- page: 2 -->

2025 was a year of growth and of deliberate investment. Revenue rose 18.4% to
EUR 142.7 million, our third consecutive year of double-digit growth. Order
intake was strong throughout the year and our closing order book of EUR 96.4
million gives good visibility into 2026.

The most significant event of the year was the commissioning of the Ostrava
machining expansion, which completed in September 2025, four months later than
originally scheduled. The delay related to the qualification of a new
five-axis machining cell and did not result in missed customer deliveries,
although it did require approximately EUR 2.1 million of unplanned expedited
freight and temporary subcontract machining during the third quarter.

We funded the expansion primarily through debt. Total borrowings increased from
EUR 21.4 million to EUR 48.3 million during the year. The board considered this
carefully and remains comfortable that the resulting leverage is appropriate
given the contracted revenue underpinning the investment. We recognise that
this represents a materially different balance sheet from the one we operated
two years ago.

We were pleased to complete our first independent cybersecurity assessment
during the year and to close the majority of its findings before year end.

## 3. Financial Review

<!-- page: 3 -->

### 3.1 Revenue and profitability

Revenue for the year was EUR 142.7 million (2024: EUR 120.5 million), an
increase of 18.4%. Gross margin was 27.1% (2024: 29.3%). The reduction in gross
margin reflects the expedited freight and subcontract machining costs incurred
during the Ostrava commissioning delay, together with input cost inflation in
copper and aluminium that the company was able to pass through only partially
during the year.

Operating profit was EUR 11.9 million (2024: EUR 12.8 million). Profit before
tax was EUR 8.4 million (2024: EUR 11.6 million), the reduction reflecting
increased finance costs following the expansion financing.

### 3.2 Customer concentration

Revenue remains concentrated in a small number of long-standing customers. In
2025, the two largest customers together accounted for 62% of consolidated
revenue (2024: 58%; 2023: 51%). The largest single customer accounted for 39%
of revenue (2024: 36%).

The company's two largest customers are served under multi-year framework
agreements. The agreement with the largest customer runs to December 2027 and
contains a rolling twelve-month volume commitment. The agreement with the
second-largest customer expires in June 2026 and is currently under
renegotiation. Management expects renewal on broadly similar commercial terms
but no agreement has been signed as at the date of this report.

Directors consider the concentration to be a consequence of the company's
strategy of deep integration with a small number of platform customers, which
has been a significant driver of the growth reported above.

### 3.3 Financial position

<!-- page: 4 -->

Total borrowings at 31 December 2025 were EUR 48.3 million (2024: EUR 21.4
million). Net debt was EUR 39.8 million (2024: EUR 14.9 million). The ratio of
net debt to EBITDA was 2.4x (2024: 1.0x).

The company's principal facility is a EUR 55.0 million senior term and
revolving facility agreement entered into in March 2025, maturing March 2030.
The facility contains a net leverage covenant of 3.25x tested semi-annually and
an interest cover covenant of 3.0x. The company was in compliance with both
covenants at the December 2025 test date, with net leverage of 2.4x and
interest cover of 4.1x.

Cash and cash equivalents at year end were EUR 8.5 million (2024: EUR 6.5
million). Undrawn committed facilities were EUR 12.0 million. The directors
consider available liquidity to be adequate for the company's requirements for
at least the twelve months from the date of approval of these accounts.

### 3.4 Going concern

The directors have reviewed cash flow forecasts covering the period to
December 2027, including a downside scenario assuming the loss of the
second-largest customer at contract expiry with no replacement volume. Under
that downside scenario the company would remain within its covenants until the
June 2027 test date, at which point headroom would be limited. The directors
have concluded that the going concern basis remains appropriate, and note that
the company retains the ability to defer discretionary capital expenditure of
approximately EUR 6 million in such a scenario.

## 4. Operations

<!-- page: 5 -->

### 4.1 Supply chain

The company sources from 214 suppliers. Purchases are concentrated: the ten
largest suppliers accounted for 71% of direct materials spend in 2025.

Two components are single-sourced. A specialist copper alloy extrusion used in
the company's principal product family is sourced from a single supplier in
northern Italy, which has supplied Northstar since 2014. A proprietary thermal
interface material is sourced from a single qualified supplier in South Korea.
Requalification of an alternative source for either component is estimated by
management to take between nine and fourteen months.

The company held 71 days of inventory at year end (2024: 54 days), an increase
that management describes as a deliberate response to supply chain conditions
and to the single-source positions described above.

### 4.2 Operational incidents

There were no lost-time safety incidents during the year. The Ostrava
commissioning delay described in the Chief Executive's Review is the only
operational matter the directors consider material to this report.

## 5. Directors' Report

<!-- page: 6 -->

### 5.1 Board composition

The board comprises six directors: the Chief Executive, the Chief Financial
Officer, two directors nominated by Stichting Northstar Holding, one director
nominated by Meridian Growth Partners IV LP, and one independent non-executive
director appointed in November 2025.

Prior to November 2025 the board had no independent non-executive directors.
The board is chaired by a director nominated by Stichting Northstar Holding,
who is a member of the founding family.

### 5.2 Committees

The audit committee comprises two directors. It met twice during 2025 (2024:
twice). The independent non-executive director joined the audit committee in
November 2025 and has been appointed as its chair with effect from
January 2026. The board has resolved that the audit committee will meet at
least four times during 2026.

There is no separate risk committee. Risk matters are considered by the board
as a whole.

### 5.3 Internal audit

The company does not maintain an internal audit function. The board reviewed
this position in October 2025 and resolved to engage an external provider to
deliver a co-sourced internal audit programme, with the first engagement
scheduled for the second quarter of 2026.

### 5.4 Auditor's report

<!-- page: 7 -->

The financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025 were audited by
Van Dijk & Peters Accountants N.V., who issued an unqualified opinion dated
14 March 2026.

The auditor's management letter identified two significant deficiencies in
internal control: (i) the absence of consistent segregation of duties in the
purchase-to-pay process at the Ostrava facility, and (ii) the absence of a
formal, documented review of manual journal entries above the company's
posting threshold. Management has accepted both findings. Remediation of the
first is in progress with a target date of June 2026; remediation of the
second was completed in February 2026.

The prior year management letter identified three significant deficiencies, of
which two have been closed.

## 6. Legal and Regulatory

There were no material legal proceedings outstanding at the reporting date. The
company received no regulatory findings or penalties during the year.

A commercial dispute with a former distributor in Turkey was settled in
May 2025 for EUR 0.4 million, fully provided in the 2024 accounts.
