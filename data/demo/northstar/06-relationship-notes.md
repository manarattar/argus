## 1. Relationship Background

Northstar has been a client since 2016. The relationship comprises a working
capital facility (participating), cash management, and FX hedging. Northstar is
not a borrower under the March 2025 senior facility, which was arranged by
another institution.

The relationship has been straightforward throughout. No payment delays, no
covenant issues raised with us, no unusual requests.

## 2. Meeting Notes

### 2.1 Meeting with CFO, 11 February 2026

<!-- page: 2 -->

Met the CFO at Eindhoven. Discussion covered FY2025 results and the outlook.

The CFO was direct about the leverage increase and did not present it as
anything other than a significant change. Her framing was that the board took a
deliberate decision to fund capacity ahead of contracted demand, that the
Ostrava commissioning delay cost more than expected, and that FY2026 is the year
the investment has to start paying back.

On the June 2026 contract renewal she was less comfortable. Her words were that
the renewal was "very likely but not done", that the customer had asked for a
price concession in the region of 4%, and that Northstar had not yet decided
how to respond. She did not commit to a timeline for resolution.

I asked what happens if the renewal does not complete. She said the company
would defer the second phase of the Ostrava programme and had modelled
remaining within covenants, which is consistent with what the accounts say. She
also volunteered that the company would need to reduce headcount at Ostrava in
that scenario, which is not in the accounts.

### 2.2 Site visit, Ostrava, 4 March 2026

<!-- page: 3 -->

Visited the Ostrava facility with the operations director. The expansion is
visibly complete and running. Utilisation was described as approximately 60% of
the expanded capacity, which is in line with the ramp plan.

The new independent non-executive director was on site the same day conducting
her own induction visit. She spent time on the shop floor and with the site
finance team rather than only with senior management. My impression, for what a
single observation is worth, is that the appointment is being taken seriously
rather than treated as a formality.

### 2.3 Call with CEO, 27 March 2026

Short call. The CEO confirmed the internal audit provider had been selected and
said the first engagement would begin in Q2 2026 as planned. He was keen to
emphasise the cybersecurity remediation, which he described as the board's
priority through the winter.

He raised, unprompted, that he was "not satisfied" with the pace of the Ostrava
segregation-of-duties remediation and had asked the CFO to bring it forward.

## 3. Relationship Manager's Assessment

<!-- page: 4 -->

My view, which should be weighed as a single relationship perspective and not as
independent evidence:

Management is credible and has been consistently straight with us, including on
matters that do not reflect well on them. The CFO's account of the leverage
decision and the CEO's unprompted comment on the segregation-of-duties delay are
both examples. In eight years I have not known this management team to present a
situation more favourably than it was.

The governance changes look real rather than cosmetic, on the limited evidence
of the March site visit.

The single issue that concerns me is the June 2026 renewal. It is 23% of
revenue, it expires in weeks, there is no signed agreement, and the customer has
opened a price discussion. Management's confidence may well be justified — they
know the customer far better than we do — but confidence is not a signed
contract, and the company's own downside model shows how tight the position
becomes if it does not land.

## 4. Recommended Actions

1. Request written confirmation of the June 2026 renewal status by 31 May 2026.
2. Bring the next review forward to Q3 2026 rather than the annual cycle.
3. No change to facility terms recommended at this time.
