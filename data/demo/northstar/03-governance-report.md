# Northstar Manufacturing B.V. — Governance Review

> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT.** Prepared for the ARGUS demonstration
> corpus. Northstar Manufacturing B.V. does not exist.

Prepared by: Corporate Governance Assessment Team
Date: 28 March 2026
Basis: statutory filings, board minutes FY2024–FY2025, auditor management
letters, management interviews conducted 3–7 March 2026

## 1. Scope and Basis

This review covers board composition and independence, committee structure and
activity, internal control environment, and the status of prior findings. It
does not cover remuneration, which was outside the agreed scope.

Management cooperated fully with the review and provided all documents
requested, with one exception noted at section 4.3.

## 2. Board Composition and Independence

### 2.1 Current composition

As at the date of this review the board comprises six directors:

| Role | Nominated by | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | Stichting Northstar Holding | No |
| Chief Executive | Executive | No |
| Chief Financial Officer | Executive | No |
| Non-executive director | Stichting Northstar Holding | No |
| Non-executive director | Meridian Growth Partners IV LP | No |
| Non-executive director | — | Yes (appointed Nov 2025) |

One of six directors is independent. The chair is not independent and is a
member of the founding family, which holds 54% of ordinary shares through
Stichting Northstar Holding.

### 2.2 Assessment

<!-- page: 2 -->

For the whole of FY2023 and FY2024, and for ten of the twelve months of FY2025,
the board had no independent director. Every board decision taken in that
period, including the March 2025 decision to increase borrowings from EUR 21.4
million to a EUR 55.0 million facility, was taken by a board composed entirely
of shareholder nominees and executives.

The November 2025 appointment is a material improvement and appears to have been
made on the initiative of the board rather than in response to an external
requirement. The appointee has relevant sector experience and a background in
audit.

However, a single independent director on a six-member board cannot outvote the
shareholder nominees on any matter, and the chair remains a family nominee.
The structural position is improved but not resolved.

## 3. Committee Structure and Activity

### 3.1 Audit committee

<!-- page: 3 -->

The audit committee met twice in FY2024 and twice in FY2025. Both years fall
below the four meetings that would be expected for a company of this size and
complexity, and below the frequency the company itself has now adopted for 2026.

Until November 2025 the audit committee comprised two directors, neither of whom
was independent. The independent director joined the committee in November 2025
and chairs it from January 2026. Two meetings have been held under the new
chair as at the date of this review, and the committee minutes show a
noticeably expanded agenda including, for the first time, a standing item on
control deficiencies.

### 3.2 Risk committee

There is no risk committee. Risk is considered by the board as a whole. Board
minutes for FY2025 record risk as a substantive agenda item at three of eight
meetings.

### 3.3 Assessment

The committee structure was materially below expectation throughout the period
under review. The changes made from November 2025 address the most significant
weaknesses — independence of the audit committee chair and meeting frequency —
but they are recent, and there is not yet enough operating history to conclude
that they are effective.

## 4. Internal Control Environment

### 4.1 Internal audit

<!-- page: 4 -->

The company has no internal audit function and has not had one at any point in
its history. In October 2025 the board resolved to engage an external provider
for a co-sourced internal audit programme, with the first engagement scheduled
for Q2 2026. A provider had been selected but not appointed as at the date of
this review.

For a company with revenue of EUR 142.7 million, two production sites in
separate jurisdictions and 612 employees, the absence of any internal audit
capability is a significant gap in the control environment.

### 4.2 Auditor findings

The FY2025 management letter from Van Dijk & Peters Accountants N.V. identified
two significant deficiencies:

1. **Segregation of duties, purchase-to-pay, Ostrava.** At the Ostrava facility,
   the same individuals were able to raise purchase orders, receipt goods and
   approve supplier invoices. The auditor identified no evidence of loss, but
   noted the control was capable of concealing one. Remediation is in progress
   with a target date of June 2026.

2. **Review of manual journal entries.** There was no formal documented review
   of manual journal entries above the company's posting threshold. Remediation
   completed February 2026 and confirmed by the auditor.

The FY2024 management letter identified three significant deficiencies, of which
two are now closed. The one that remains open across both years is the
segregation-of-duties finding at Ostrava, which has therefore been outstanding
for more than twelve months.

### 4.3 Documentation limitation

<!-- page: 5 -->

Board minutes for the meeting of 12 February 2025, at which the facility
agreement was first discussed, were provided in summary form only. Management
stated that full minutes for that meeting were not prepared. This review has
therefore been unable to assess what alternatives to the debt-funded expansion
were considered by the board, or on what basis.

This is recorded as a limitation of scope, not as a finding.

## 6. Overall Conclusion

<!-- page: 6 -->

Governance at Northstar has been materially weaker than would be expected for a
company of its size and financial profile, and it was weakest during precisely
the period in which the company took its most consequential financial decision.

The direction of travel since November 2025 is clearly positive and the changes
made are the right ones. Three qualifications apply. The changes are recent and
largely unproven. The most significant structural feature — a controlling
shareholder nominating the chair and a majority of the board — is unchanged. And
the one control deficiency that has persisted across two audit cycles is the one
with the most direct fraud exposure.

A reviewer relying on this report should treat the remediation programme as
credible but not yet demonstrated. The appropriate response is a shorter review
cycle rather than a conclusion in either direction.
